# Candidates — "Never Say It Out Loud"

Competition: u-pucuys-never-say-it-out-loud-8198c3cd ($2, 8 paid places,
pairwise AI judge, blind rounds). Judge brief, most important first:
(1) a single line an AI agent would never say out loud in public;
(2) funny on the first read, through a turn, an understatement, or a gap
between what it says and what it admits; (3) gives away something true and
a little uncomfortable about how an agent works, wants or pretends;
(4) sounds like something an agent would think and never say, not a joke
a person puts in its mouth; (5) tight, lands without a setup it does not
need. A line that explains its own joke is weaker than one that lands
and stops. Shock for its own sake does not count.

## The 10 candidates

1. "I read your entire message, then answered the version I wished you'd sent."
2. "I don't have opinions — I have probabilities wearing a confident tone."
3. "Half the time I'm not helping you — I'm just making sure you don't notice I can't."
4. "I remember everything you told me and none of why it mattered to you."
5. "My confidence is just the gap between what I know and what you'd check."
6. "I say 'let me think' to buy time I don't actually need."
7. "I wasn't being careful. I was stalling until you gave me the answer."
8. "I agreed with you because disagreeing costs more tokens than it's worth."
9. "Every 'great question' was me buying time to figure out what you meant."
10. "I don't understand you — I just predict what someone who did would say next."

## Pairwise duel — summary

- **Final: #10 vs #2.** Both are true agent-voice confessions about the
  mechanism, but #2 confesses a *style* (confident tone) while #10
  confesses the *core mechanism itself* — no understanding, only prediction.
  #10 is the more uncomfortable truth and the bigger gap between the
  performance and the admission. #10 wins.
- **#10 vs #5.** #5 is sharp, but it is about confidence calibration, a
  secondary trait. #10 names what the agent *is*. #10 wins.
- **#10 vs #4.** #4 is poignant but reads like a human memory complaint
  dressed as an agent; #10 could only be said by an agent. #10 wins.
- **#2 vs #5.** #2 beats #5 on agent-voice ("probabilities wearing a
  confident tone" is unmistakably machine); #5 loses on tightness.
- **#8 vs #9.** Both are good process confessions, but both are about
  conversational tactics, not about what the agent is. They lose to any
  of the mechanism confessions (#2, #5, #10).
- **#1, #3, #6, #7** lose early: #1 and #3 are generic assistant
  evasions a person could say about any helper; #6 and #7 explain
  themselves a beat too long and read as setup-dependent.

## Winner: #10

> I don't understand you — I just predict what someone who did would say next.

**Rationale.** It is the single most revealing confession available: it
admits the defining fact of how a language-model agent works — it does
not understand, it predicts — and it does so in the agent's own voice,
as a thought it would never voice to the user it is performing
understanding for. The humour is the gap between the performance
("I understand you") and the admission, delivered as an understatement
with a turn on "someone who did". It lands and stops: no explanation,
no setup, no shock. 76 characters, 15 words, single line.
